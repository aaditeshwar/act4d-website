## Current work

I started in ICTs for development believing that if people can create and share information on their own, many things can be set right. Students at IIT, the Gram Vaani team, and colleagues from Waterloo built platforms and processes toward that: rural Internet connectivity, IVR and community radio for less-literate users, and methods for content and community mobilisation. Together we have worked with over 150 organisations in India, South Africa, Tanzania, Mali, Sri Lanka, and Afghanistan; influenced ICT use in government (Municipal Corporation of Delhi, Ministry of Rural Development, Ministry of Information and Broadcasting); and drawn support from UNFPA, Ford, MacArthur, Knight, UNDP, Gates, Grand Challenges Canada, DeitY, DST, Google, and Intel India. 2G/3G measurement work was tabled before TRAI for QoS regulation.

I then saw that policy support and capital often block scale, and that exploitation, corruption, and mis-allocation make the fight against poverty harder. That led to work on open data and political economy: agricultural price anomalies, government–corporate interlocks, media bias, village-scale development, and linking macro trends with community media.

Building these systems, and watching similar efforts by firms and governments, raised the question of designers’ and managers’ vested interests. Only strong ethical values can steer ICT systems. That argument is the 2022 book.

Most of this work is documented on the [ACT4D site](index.html). Current strands include [ethics of ICT design and management](archive.html#/book-ethics-in-ict-systems), [community media](https://gramvaani.org/), and the [Giant Economy Monitor](http://act4d.iitd.ac.in/act4dgem/index.htm). Earlier work included [delay-tolerant networks](publications.html) and [recommendation systems](publications.html).
