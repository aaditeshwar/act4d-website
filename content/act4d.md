# ACT4D — Appropriate Computing Technologies for Development

*A research group in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering, IIT Delhi, led by Prof. Aaditeshwar Seth.*

## About

Led by Aaditeshwar Seth, professor at IIT Delhi's Department of Computer Science and Engineering since 2009, the group works on four broad, connected areas:

- **Environment and natural resource management for community-based development** — using geospatial data, satellite imagery, and field knowledge to help rural communities plan around water, land, and forests as climate pressures grow.
- **Analysis of factors impeding socio-economic development in rural areas** — tracking how policy, media, and political economy shape (or stall) development outcomes across Indian districts.
- **Participatory information sharing systems in low-literacy environments** — voice- and mobile-based platforms that let people with limited literacy or connectivity produce and consume local information on their own terms.
- **Ethics of information systems** — how technology design and the management of technology platforms can either entrench or dismantle existing inequalities.

*Talks introducing each of these areas are linked from the [source profile page](https://www.cse.iitd.ernet.in/~aseth/).*

## Major Achievements

**[Gram Vaani](https://gramvaani.org/)** (2009–present) — A social enterprise co-founded by Aaditeshwar Seth that runs voice-based community media (Mobile Vaani) across rural India, giving low-literacy, low-connectivity communities a platform to share information, hold institutions accountable, and organize collectively.

**[CoRE Stack](https://core-stack.org/)** (2022–present) — A digital public infrastructure for climate adaptation and natural resource management, co-created with 20+ civil society organizations across 9 Indian states, combining a nationally standardized micro-watershed registry, field tools for community "landscape stewards," and a neurosymbolic diagnostics engine to turn geospatial and community data into demands rural communities take to their Gram Sabhas and government schemes.

**[*Technology and (Dis)Empowerment: A Call to Technologists*](https://www.emerald.com/insight/publication/doi/10.1108/9781803823935)** (Emerald Publishing, 2022) — A book distilling over a decade of work on the relationship between technology, political economy, and social development, arguing that technology's design and management should aim explicitly at power-based equality rather than efficiency or profit alone. (See also the author's own [summary on ICTworks](https://www.ictworks.org/technology-and-disempowerment/) and [on Gram Vaani](https://www.gramvaani.org/technology-and-disempowerment-a-call-to-technologists/).)

## Key Ideologies

The following document lays out the thinking that guides the group's work, and is worth reading in full.

**[Technology as a tool to overturn unjust structures](https://www.cse.iitd.ernet.in/~aseth/act.html)** — the ideas behind *Technology and (Dis)Empowerment* hold that technology's role should be "to overturn unjust societal structures to empower the weak and oppressed," not to reproduce them. This means: setting clear ends alongside means (a "do no harm" guardrail isn't a compass); building explicitly toward power-based equality; embedding ethics into both the design *and* the ongoing management of a technology, since most harms surface only once a system meets the real world; and ensuring the people a technology is meant to serve are actually involved in designing and managing it.

## Publications and Research Outputs

The group's publication record (in full at [cse.iitd.ernet.in/~aseth/publications.html](https://www.cse.iitd.ernet.in/~aseth/publications.html)) traces the same arc as the "Current work" on the main page, extended by several years of subsequent work on CoRE Stack.

The earliest and largest body of work grew out of Gram Vaani itself: a long run of ICTD, COMPASS, and CSCW papers on designing, running, and evaluating voice- and IVR-based participatory media platforms in rural India — from early design lessons (*Design Lessons from Creating a Mobile-based Community Media Platform in Rural India*, ICTD 2016) through community mobilization and grievance-redressal studies, to more recent work on AI-assisted moderation and automated question-answering for these platforms, and on the credibility and recommendation models (dating back to Seth's PhD) that decide what content participants see. A parallel strand studied the platforms' real-world effects — on maternal and child health behaviour, welfare-scheme grievance redressal, and civic participation — much of it conducted jointly with the Gram Vaani team.

Alongside this, from around 2018 the group turned to the political economy of development itself: analyses of media bias, corporate–government interlocks, and ideology in Indian mass media, and the *Giant Economy Monitor* built in 2019 to track policy bias. This line of thinking, together with the ethics-of-technology strand (*The Limits of Design in Ensuring Responsible Outcomes from Technology*, ICTD 2020; *A New Paradigm to Accommodate Ethical Foundations in the Design and Management of Digital Platforms*, 2019), fed directly into the 2022 book, *Technology and (Dis)Empowerment: A Call to Technologists*, and into shorter position pieces such as *Missing Publics in Digital Public Goods: The Need to Build Technologies for Communities* ([The India Forum](https://www.theindiaforum.in/forum/bringing-back-public-digital-public-goods), 2022), which argued that digital public infrastructure needs to be built as "technologies for communities" rather than imposed top-down — the same critique of individualized, Aadhaar-style digital ID that runs through the book's epilogue.

From around 2019 the group also began using satellite imagery and remote sensing to track socio-economic development directly — land-use change near mining sites, city growth patterns, district development models from census data, and datasets like *IndiaSat* for land-cover classification — building the geospatial and machine-learning capability that CoRE Stack now runs on. The 2022–2026 work is the direct continuation of this line, described in the group's own project blogs and captured in a "Chapter 12" retrospective written as a sequel to the 2022 book: CoRE Stack combines this geospatial layer with a nationally standardized **micro-watershed registry**, a field toolset (**Know Your Landscape**, **Commons Connect**) used by trained community "landscape stewards" to turn data into Detailed Project Reports for Gram Sabhas and schemes like MGNREGA, and a **neurosymbolic evidence-card engine** ("CoRE Insights") that uses large language models only to draft candidate rules from literature — with humans curating them into deterministic, inspectable logic, rather than letting an LLM diagnose directly. By mid-2026 this had grown to 20+ partner CSOs across 9 states and roughly 90 tehsils, 430+ trained stewards, and integration work underway with government portals such as Jharkhand's Yuktadhara. A related empirical study, *An In-depth Analysis of Caste-based Inequity in Welfare Allocation of the National Rural Employment Guarantee Scheme in India* ([Mittal, Sheth & Seth, ICTD 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3700794.3700815); [PDF](https://www.cse.iitd.ernet.in/~aseth/caste-based-ictd2024.pdf)), used MGNREGA MIS data and fieldwork in Bihar and Jharkhand to locate caste-based inequities in India's employment guarantee scheme down to the panchayat level — the same kind of caste-disaggregated evidence CoRE Stack's field tools now surface directly to communities.
